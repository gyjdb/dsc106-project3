Data and method

Source

Monthly near-surface air temperature (tas) from the CMIP6 public archive on Google Cloud: the historical run plus four future scenarios, using one run from each of models. Coastlines and city locations come from Natural Earth.

Which models

In the archive, 38 models provide all five experiments, and 25 of them have a transient climate response inside the IPCC AR6 likely range of 1.4–2.2 °C (Hausfather et al., 2022). We use of those 25, with no two from the same CMIP6 institution ID. The screen leaves out the “hot models” that warm faster than the evidence supports. Models used: .

Transformations

Each model is regridded to a shared 2.5° grid with area weighting and averaged by year. Warming is measured per grid cell against that model’s own 1850–1900 mean, then smoothed with a 20-year running mean. A place “arrives” in the first year after the baseline that this average reaches the level and stays there for at least 20 more years, or to the end of the data, so a short warm spell does not count. The year shown is the middle of that first 20-year period.

Reading it carefully

The map shows the median of the models’ arrival years, one block per grid cell. A city’s value describes its whole cell, up to about 280 km across, not the city itself. Years up to today are model estimates, not observations. “Not by 2090” means the level was not reached and held: the last 20-year period is 2080–2099, and a place can touch a level without staying there. Because the rule looks ahead, arrival years can differ between scenarios from the 1980s on, even though the scenarios share history through 2014.
